Ramp Hook vs Rigid V

Which guide gets a qualifier Auto to 3 TIPs with both robots LEAVE and PARK? The Flat Intake (no guide) against three Rigid Vs and the Ramp Hook, through the three current qualifier Autos: 60 runs each, filmed TIP timing and rolling, one tile surface. Side Rails are left for later.

What the simulator says

  • The Rigid V is the guide to build. With the partner that shoots, the 45° Rigid V on a route that PARKs gets 3 TIPs in 33 of 60, both robots PARK in 58, and is touched by a falling piece (G409) in only 3 runs. A longer V (18 in across, 30°) gets 3 TIPs in 41 of 60 and PARK in 57, with G409 in 6. But its flaps cross the centre line on both Stages routes, so those routes would need redrawing for it.
  • The Ramp Hook scores about the same and gets touched in half the matches. 66.3 points, 3 TIPs in 35 of 60, PARK in 51, and G409 in 30 of 60 (41 on the angled Stages route). The spill is still in the air when the hook is down: it lands on the hook and on the robot’s own front face. Moving the waiting spot only trades one for the other. A ramp shape could fix the hook’s share, but not the face’s.
  • Neither guide gets 3 TIPs when the partner stages. Every guide scores about 51 (angled partner) or 47 (wall partner), with TIP 2 at 20–21 s and no time for TIP 3. The Rigid V picks up more of TIP 1’s spill (2.5 of 4 against 1.9) but doesn’t score more. Against the wall partner it scores less (39–42), because it doesn’t fit the west lane and goes through the tunnel. That needs a new plan, not a guide.
  • PARK was a route problem. On the partner-shoots Auto, the Rigid V’s route went back for a third GARDEN load whenever TIP 3 was late, and lost PARK in 25 of 60 runs; that load never made TIP 3. Parking instead (qual-right-o3-rigid-v-park) keeps every TIP and PARKs in 58 of 60. The Flat Intake’s current route has the same kind of gap: PARK in 13 of 60.

The Ramp Hook

Why it gets touched, wherever it waits

Every touch is TIP 2’s spill still in the air, 14.4–15.1 s in, 1–7 in up and falling at up to 160 in/s. It lands on the robot’s front face (8.7 in ahead of its centre) or on the hook (14 in ahead). Moving the robot closer puts more on the face; moving it back puts more on the hook. Moving the robot can’t fix it. The hook’s shape, or when it comes down, might.
