Which guide gets a qualifier Auto to 3 TIPs with both robots LEAVE and PARK? The Flat Intake (no guide) against three Rigid Vs and the Ramp Hook, each on a route drawn for it, through the three current qualifier Autos: 60 runs each, filmed TIP timing and rolling, one tile surface. Side Rails are left for later.

What the simulator says

  • Each guide now runs a route drawn for it. The first run put each guide on the Flat Intake’s routes, and the logs showed it. The Ramp Hook never came down on the Stages Autos, and the Rigid V backed into the wall partner’s row. The routes are redrawn below; the grey rows are the first run.
  • With the partner that shoots, the long Rigid V (18 in, 30°) does best. 68.8 points, 3 TIPs in 41 of 60, both PARK in 57, G409 in 6. The 45° V gets 66.2 and 33. The Ramp Hook gets 66.3 and 35, but PARKs in only 51 and is touched in 30.
  • With a staging partner, the Ramp Hook does best: its arm finally does its job. Waiting at the south spot with the hook down for TIP 1, it keeps 3.1 of 4 (Flat Intake: 1.9). Catch plus the staged row is then 8, so TIP 2 comes off the row. With the angled partner it comes by 17.5 s in 28 of 59 runs (Flat Intake: 10). With the wall partner it comes in all 60 runs, at 18.7 s (median) instead of 21.5. 55.7 points with the angled partner and 51.0 with the wall partner, against the Flat Intake’s 51.6 and 47.3. It is touched by the falling spill in 28 of 60.
  • Nothing reaches TIP 3 with a staging partner yet, but the Ramp Hook against the wall partner is close. It is loading the GARDEN for TIP 3 as AUTO ends.
  • G409 is the Ramp Hook’s price everywhere (28–30 runs of 60), against 0–9 for the Vs. Half the touches land on the robot’s own front face, so a better ramp alone won’t fix it. Over the three Autos the totals are level: the long V 57.4 average points, the Ramp Hook 57.7, the 45° V 55.7, the Flat Intake 51.2. The simulator doesn’t charge for G409.
  • The wide V (20 in) is out. Turning on the CELL’s line (x 57.5), its flap tips swing over the centre line, and it would have to fold for the start.

The log review

What each guide’s route changed

Ramp Hook, Stages

The hook is one right arm (doc/ramp-hook.md), which faces the centre line only at the south end. It can serve TIP 1, never TIP 2. Its route now fires from S_FIRE, near the HIVE, and stops at the south hook spot (face 37.5 in from the wall) as TIP 1 starts. It holds 1 s after the CELL settles, then runs the baseline. Before, it drove off after TIP 1 and waited at the north spot for TIP 2 with the hook up.

Rigid V, wall partner’s row

The baseline backs west along y 118 and slides north beside the row. The V’s flap tips shove it, so it picked up 0–1 of 4. Face first at an angle stops at the row’s south end and pushes the rest off (1 of 4). Sweeping up the row, square to it, stopping at each piece, takes 4 of 4: the pieces chain into the north wall and the V keeps them in its mouth.

Rigid V, PARK

When TIP 3 was late, its partner-shoots route went back for a third GARDEN load. That is its last card, which the endgame guard never cuts, so it lost PARK in 25 of 60 runs and the load never made TIP 3. It PARKs instead now. Cutting the sweep through TIP 1’s leftovers (0–1 picked up) changed nothing (68.8 either way).

Long and wide Vs, centre line

Their flap tips swing 13.7–14.1 in out as the robot turns. From x 57.5 that reaches over the centre line (x 70.6). The long V turns 2 in further west out of the tunnel now, and runs clean. The wide V still crosses on a later turn.

The Ramp Hook

Why it gets touched, wherever it waits

Every touch is TIP 2’s spill still in the air, 14.4–15.1 s in, 1–7 in up and falling at up to 160 in/s. It lands on the robot’s front face (8.7 in ahead of its centre) or on the hook (14 in ahead). Moving the robot closer puts more on the face; moving it back puts more on the hook. Moving the robot can’t fix it. The hook’s shape, or when it comes down, might.
