Keep Four Deep Dive

Can our robot pick up 4 of every spill, carry them to the other CELL and fire them, so every qualifier ends with 3 TIPs and both robots LEAVE and PARK? 4,000+ simulated matches: every Auto in the repository, and the qualifier Autos with every body shape we have, 20 runs each on normal tiles and on tiles with 3× the friction.

What the simulator says

  • Keeping 4 is the lever, when the partner shoots. With a partner that fires its 4, picking up all 4 of TIP 2’s spill leads to 3 TIPs in 87–96% of matches; picking up fewer, in 36–49%.
  • The hook keeps 4 most reliably, where the robot can wait at its spot: TIP 1 of the left-start Auto, 3.9 of 4 picked up on average and all 4 in 12 runs of 20 (plain: 2.1, all 4 in 2). That Auto goes from 54 to 69 points and from 3 to 16 runs with 3 TIPs. Its cost: falling pieces touch it (G409) in about 7 runs of 20.
  • Funnel flaps or a rigid V do it without moving parts, by widening the 14 in intake’s mouth: about 3.4 of 4 kept, few G409 touches (0–4 runs of 20). Best of all bodies with the right-start partner (funnel flaps 72.5 / 73.5 points, 3 TIPs in 17–18 of 20); with the left-start partner about 64, behind the hook.
  • No body gets 3 TIPs when the partner stages or does nothing. TIP 2 comes at 17–23 s in every version; TIP 3 would need about 8 more pieces in 9 s. That takes a different route or a faster robot, not a different body.

The hook

What it does, and its touches

Hook or route?

The hook routes fire TIP 1 from closer in and hold 1 s for the spill. Without a hook, that route alone takes the left-start Auto from 54 to 59 points; the hook takes it to 69. Most of the gain is the hook.

Why only a dual hook works both ends

At the north end the arm facing the centre line is the robot’s left; at the south end, its right. The left-start and stages Autos catch at both ends, so they need an arm each side: two hooks on their own hinges, the one facing the centre line coming down.

The touches

About 7 runs of 20 on the left-start Auto. They hit the face and the crossbeam: these spills (our preloads with 3 NECTAR, or the partner’s 4 with our catch) land wider than the 9.5 in between them. The plain robot waiting at the same spot is touched as often. Moving the spot only moves them (table below).

A late hook doesn’t help

Waiting further back and lowering the hook 1.5 s after the TIP, once the spill has landed, avoids the touches, but by then the spill has scattered: it scores exactly what the plain robot does. The hook works only when it is down as the pieces land. How far they roll after landing is a guess the spill test would measure.

For the meeting

What to decide and test

Decide

  • Funnel flaps or rigid V: the safe body, good with both shooting partners, nothing that moves.
  • The dual hook: the best keeper and the best left-start Auto, but two hinged arms and G409 risk in about a third of matches.
  • Stages and does-nothing partners need a new plan (TIP 2 earlier), whatever the body.

Test on a field

  • doc/spill-test.md: where the spill lands (the simulator lands it about 6 in short of the 3 Oct films) and how far it rolls. Every hook spot moves with it.
  • Cardboard funnel flaps on the build robot: do pieces really slide in?
  • A cardboard hook at the left-start Auto’s spot: does it catch 4, and is it touched?

Guesses in every number here: the intake (it takes a piece only touching it and slow, 0.35 s each), the launcher, how pieces bounce and roll, the partner (a spring-hood robot at 40 in/s).
