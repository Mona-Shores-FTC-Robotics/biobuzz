# Spill test: where a TIP's pieces land and where they stop

**Why.** The simulator decides where to park a robot to catch a TIP's spill, and which robot shape is worth
building. It already matches the 3 Oct films for when and where spilled pieces first hit the tiles. But how far
they roll after that, and how they bounce off walls, are still guesses: the simulator says half the pieces end up
more than 3 ft from where they landed. This test measures that, and the HIVE's swing time with it. It takes
about an hour for 20 TIPs with three people.

**What you get back.** Your videos and photos tune the simulator's rolling and bouncing. Then the shape
pictures (`sim-review/body-shapes.png`), the parking spots and the Autos are redone on real numbers.

## You need

- The field with the HIVE set up as the Event Field Setup Guide says, and **nothing else on it**.
- 3 NECTAR (our alliance's colour) and about 12 POLLEN.
- A launcher, or a person who can toss POLLEN into the CELL the way a launcher would.
- A phone that films at 60 fps or more (120 is better), and a way to hold it high and still: a step ladder, a
  tripod on a table, or a pole. Higher is better; still is essential.
- Blue painter's tape and a tape measure.
- This sheet printed, and a pencil.

Three people: one films, one shoots, one writes the notes and resets.

## Set up once

1. **Pick one CELL and keep it.** The simulator studies the red HIVE's CELL that tips toward the red alliance
   wall. Use that one if you can, or another, and write down which.
2. **Tape a distance scale.** Lay blue tape marks on the floor every 12 in, starting at the alliance wall and
   heading straight out toward the HIVE, just beside where the pieces land (not under the CELL's opening).
   Flat tape doesn't change how pieces roll. Label 12, 24, 36, 48 and 60.
3. **Place the camera.** It should look **straight down** at the floor between the alliance wall and the
   HIVE. In frame:
   - the CELL's opening;
   - the tape scale;
   - at least two tile seams each way (they are 23.6 in apart, and the photos are measured from them);
   - as much floor to the sides as you can get, since pieces roll a long way.

   Take a test photo and check that the whole tape scale shows. Mark where the ladder or tripod stands, and **don't
   move it** between runs. If it moves, take a new test photo and note it.

## Run A: the match-start load (10 TIPs, then 10 more if you can)

For each TIP:

1. Reset: the CELL raised and empty, then 3 NECTAR placed in it the way the Field Setup Guide's match setup says.
   Clear every piece off the floor.
2. **Start filming**, and say the run number out loud (or hold up a card with it).
3. Shoot POLLEN in one at a time until the CELL tips. Count them.
4. Keep filming until **nothing on the floor is moving** (10–15 s). Nobody touches a piece.
5. Stop the video, and take one photo from the camera spot.
6. Fill in the row:
   - How many POLLEN it took to tip.
   - How many pieces stayed in the CELL.
   - Anything odd: a piece hit a person, stopped against the tape roll, or bounced off something that won't be
     on a match field. **Keep that run anyway**, just note it.
7. Pick everything up, and reset for the next run.

## Run B: POLLEN only (10 TIPs, then 10 more if you can)

Same as Run A, but start each TIP with the raised CELL empty and shoot POLLEN until it tips. Count them, since
this tells us whether it really takes about 8. **Shoot them in; don't place them by hand.** The simulator places
them against the back, and that is probably why its POLLEN spill leans to one side.

## How many runs

- **10 of each** is enough to tell whether the simulator's rolling is roughly right.
- **20 of each** pins down the edges of the spill: where the 90% box and the farthest pieces really are.

Do 10 of each first and send them over. The answer will say whether 10 more would change anything.

## Sending the data

Name the files with the run, like `A01.mov`, `A01.jpg`, `B07.mov`, `B07.jpg`. Take a photo of the filled-in notes
too. Put everything in one folder (a shared drive is fine) and add the link to issue #156. If a video is too big,
the photo plus the notes still help. The video adds where each piece first lands, how long the HIVE took to
swing, and where everything stops, all from the same TIP.

## Later: a cardboard robot (after picking a shape)

Once one or two shapes look worth building (`sim-review/body-shapes.png`), tape cardboard walls or flaps of the
drawn sizes to any 18 in box or a drivetrain. Park it where the picture shows, and repeat Run A with the same
camera. For each TIP, note:

- how many pieces end up in front of the robot, or between its walls;
- whether any **falling** piece touched the robot before hitting the floor (G409), and on which part: the chassis,
  or a wall or flap. The video shows this best.
